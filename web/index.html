How this estimate works

Source video bitrate × codec factor × output/source pixel count × output/source frame rate × selected percentage. Example: 4K60 at 40 Mbps becomes a 5 Mbps baseline at 1080p30 with the same codec. This is a bitrate estimate, not a measured quality match.

Archiver uses a consistent encoding preset for tests and final output, then adjusts compression to meet your quality target.

How testing works

Archiver checks motion in short sections, selects varied clips, then uses ab-av1 to find settings that meet your XPSNR target. It checks each clip separately, including additional clips kept out of the initial search.

Similar recordings: Test one representative video, review the result, then apply its settings to the group. This works best for footage from the same game or camera with similar lighting, motion and recording settings. The longest video is selected automatically; choose another if it better represents your group.

Mixed footage: Choose Each selected video. Archiver tests each file and adds successful results to the Encoding Queue with its own settings. Whether encoding starts immediately depends on your queue setting.

Test depth changes how much footage is checked, not your quality target. Fast checks less footage and can miss difficult scenes. Extensive provides broader coverage and takes longer. Short videos use fewer clips. Runtime also depends on resolution, encoder and hardware. No sample test guarantees quality throughout a recording, and a target may not be achievable with the chosen encoder. Failed tests keep the originals and do not queue an encode.
